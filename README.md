## The Business Logic & Methodology (Plain-English Guide for Non-Technical Operators)

You do not need a data science background to understand why standard marketing reports lose money—and how this spreadsheet fixes it. Here is the core commercial logic in plain English:

### 1. The Hidden Business Problem: Why Your Best Customers Look Like "Strangers" in Shopify

Imagine you spend **$5,000 on Google Ads** and **$5,000 on Facebook Ads** in January:
- **Facebook** brings you 100 cheap leads at $50 each. They download a free brochure, never talk to sales, and buy only $2,000 worth of small accessories.
- **Google Ads** brings you only 10 expensive leads at $500 each. Because they are serious B2B buyers, they don't buy on day one. Instead, they email your sales rep, ask for a custom quote, request a product sample in February, and finally click a direct invoice link to pay **$60,000 on Shopify** in April.

**Here is the commercial blind spot that bankrupts ad budgets:**
Because 90 days passed between the January ad click and the April payment, the browser tracking cookie expired. Furthermore, the buyer paid via an emailed invoice link rather than clicking the ad again.
- **What Shopify & GA4 tell you:** Google Ads made **$0**. "Direct Traffic" made **$60,000**.
- **What your marketing team does by mistake:** They panic over Google's $500 cost-per-lead, **shut off Google Ads**, and pour all your budget into Facebook because "$50 leads look cheaper." You just killed the channel that generated $60,000 in sales.

### 2. How This Template Fixes the Blind Spot: The "Email Receipt" Matching Method

Instead of trusting fragile browser cookies that expire or get blocked by phones, this Excel template uses the one piece of data that never changes from the first inquiry to the final invoice: **the customer's email address**.

Think of it like matching three paper receipts on your desk:

- **Step 1: Pin the First Name Tag (Marketing Lead Log)**
  When `buyer@company.com` fills out a quote form on your site in January, Sheet 1 permanently stamps `" came from Google Ads "` next to their email address.
- **Step 2: Track the Sales Conversation (CRM Pipeline Sheet)**
  While your sales rep negotiates with `buyer@company.com` over February and March, Sheet 2 tracks whether the quote is in *Sample Sent*, *Negotiation*, or *Closed Won*. If deals get stuck here, you know immediately it is a **sales follow-up problem**, not a marketing traffic problem.
- **Step 3: Connect the Cash Register (Shopify Order Sheet)**
  When `buyer@company.com` finally pays $60,000 on Shopify in April, Sheet 3 imports that payment. The spreadsheet automatically looks up `buyer@company.com` back in Sheet 1, sees the `"Google Ads"` stamp from January, and hands the full **$60,000 credit back to Google Ads**.

### 3. The Commercial Decisions You Can Now Make Confidently

Once your lead log, CRM quotes, and Shopify payments are linked in one table, three operational blind spots disappear immediately:
1. **Stop cutting profitable "expensive" ads:** You see which channels bring real paying buyers months later, allowing you to scale high-ticket campaigns that competitors are too blind to keep running.
2. **Stop wasting money on "cheap" junk leads:** You instantly spot channels that generate hundreds of form fills but $0 in Shopify checkout revenue.
3. **Separate marketing quality from sales performance:** If a channel brings great leads that stall in the "Proposal Sent" stage of your CRM, you know to fix your sales quoting process rather than firing your ad agency.

### Three Analytical Traps That Catch Experienced Marketing Operators

#### Trap 1 — Optimizing for Cost Per Lead (CPL) Instead of Realized ROAS

A marketing manager compared two acquisition channels using front-end CPL metrics:

| Acquisition Channel | Reported CPL |
| --- | --- |
| Facebook Lead Ads | $31 |
| Google Search Ads | $127 |

Budget was shifted toward Facebook. Six months later, after reconciling Shopify order data:

| Acquisition Channel | Realized Shopify Revenue |
| --- | --- |
| Facebook Lead Ads | $74,000 |
| Google Search Ads | $512,000 |

**Why it failed:** Lead volume was mistaken for commercial value. The workbook enforces revenue-weighted evaluation:

```text
Marketing ROI (%) = (Attributed Shopify Revenue - Channel Ad Spend) ÷ Channel Ad Spend

```

```excel
=SUMIFS(
  Revenue_Attribution_Calc[FT_Attributed_Rev],
  Revenue_Attribution_Calc[First_Touch_Source],
  [@Channel]
)

```

---

#### Trap 2 — Trusting Default Shopify "Direct" Attribution on Delayed B2B Orders

A typical consultative B2B buyer journey looks like this:

```text
Google Search Ad → Quote Form → CRM Discovery Call → Physical Sample Shipped → Email Invoice Link → Direct Shopify Checkout

```

Because the buyer clicked an emailed invoice link 90 days later, Shopify recorded `Conversion Source = Direct`. Management falsely concluded that Google Ads produced zero sales.

**How the workbook fixes it:** It decouples acquisition identity from checkout session cookies using `XLOOKUP` against the master identity table:

```excel
=XLOOKUP(
  [@Email],
  Customer_Master[Email],
  Customer_Master[First_Touch_Source],
  "Direct / Unattributed"
)

```

---

#### Trap 3 — Auditing Marketing Traffic Without CRM Sales Stage Visibility

When close rates drop, teams often blame lead quality. However, inspecting the full funnel reveals where revenue actually leaks:

```text
Marketing Ad Click → Lead Captured → CRM Qualification → Custom Proposal → Price Negotiation → Closed Won (Shopify)

```

By pulling the latest CRM deal stage into the customer record, you can isolate whether low ROI is caused by poor leads (failing at Qualification) or pricing friction (stalling at Proposal/Negotiation):

```excel
=XLOOKUP(
  [@Email],
  CRM_Sales_Pipeline[Email],
  CRM_Sales_Pipeline[Sales_Stage],
  "Direct Checkout (Non-CRM)",
  0,
  -1
)

```

### Built-In Spreadsheet Data Validation Rules

| Column Field | Validation Rule Applied | Error Handling Behavior |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Email` | Must contain `@` and valid domain string | Record flagged & rejected from master key |
| `Lead_ID` | Unique string identifier | Conditional formatting duplicate warning |
| `Opportunity_ID` | Unique CRM deal key | Conditional formatting duplicate warning |
| `Shopify_Order_ID` | Unique transactional key | Prevents double-counting revenue |
| `Sales_Stage` | Restricted drop-down list (enumerated stages) | Data validation input alert |
| `Revenue` | Numeric value `>= 0` | Blocks negative or text strings |
| `Marketing_Cost` | Positive numeric value `> 0` | Prevents `#DIV/0!` in ROAS & ROI columns |
| `Order_Status` | Enumerated (`Paid` / `Refunded` / `Pending`) | Excludes unpaid/refunded orders from net ROAS |
| `Attribution_Source` | Must match standardized channel dictionary | Generates unmapped channel warning |
